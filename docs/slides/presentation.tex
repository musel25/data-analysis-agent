\section{Context: can an agent do a scientist's analysis?}
% ###########################################################################

\begin{frame}{Why measure this now}
\why{AI agents can now write and run code in a loop, unsupervised. So the question both papers ask is the same: can they do the part of science that is \emph{judgement}, not typing?}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The premise.} Coding agents have improved fast. It is worth finding out, carefully, how well they do \emph{real scientific research} --- and the only way to know is a \textbf{benchmark that actually informs you}.
  \pause
  \medskip
  \item \textbf{The bottleneck moved.} Sequencing got cheap; the analysis did not.
        \smallskip
        \sm{``as sequencing has scaled, downstream computation and analysis, rather than data generation, have become the central scientific bottleneck.'' --- GBP, p.\,2}
  \pause
  \medskip
  \item \textbf{The stakes are real.} Drug targets with human genetic evidence are
        \emph{``roughly 2.6 times more likely to succeed in the clinic''}. \sm{--- DDB, p.\,2}
  \pause
  \medskip
  \item \textbf{And the step is slow and expensive.} One GBP problem is \textbf{10--40 hours} of expert
        time --- \emph{``on the order of a few thousand dollars''}. \sm{--- GBP, p.\,14}
\end{itemize}
\pause
\vfill
\begin{alertblock}{So the question is worth a benchmark}
If an agent could reliably do this analysis, it would compress the slowest, most expensive step in
data-rich biology. \textbf{Can it --- and how would you ever know?}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What is actually under evaluation --- and the two papers}
\why{Neither paper evaluates a bare LLM. What is measured is a \textbf{model $+$ a harness}, inside a container.}

\begin{block}{What runs, per task}
\begin{enumerate}\footnotesize
  \item A \textbf{Docker container}: Python, the scientific stack, the data files staged on disk.
  \item A \textbf{harness} drives the loop --- sends the prompt, \textbf{executes the code the model emits}, feeds stdout back. \sm{The model never touches the machine.}
  \item The model gets the question \textbf{at a high level}, \emph{not} the method: which rows to keep, which estimator, when the evidence suffices. \textbf{No human, no hints.}
  \item It emits \textbf{one machine-checkable answer} --- GBP: a JSON object. DDB: \code{/workspace/answer.md}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}
\pause
\smallskip
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \textbf{GeneBench-Pro} \sm{(Li \& Ho, \textbf{OpenAI} --- bioRxiv, June 2026)}: \textbf{129 problems}, genomics and translational biomedicine. \gd{Graded by deterministic Python.}
  \item \textbf{DrugDiscoveryBench} \sm{(Aky\"urek, Tu et al., \textbf{Scale AI \& Phylo}, 2026)}: \textbf{82 tasks}, early-stage drug discovery. \rd{Graded by an LLM judge} against expert rubrics.
\end{itemize}
\pause
\begin{alertblock}{Keep this in your pocket: ``the model scored $X$'' means ``\emph{in this harness}, $X$''}
DDB measures it --- one model in a different harness moves \textbf{8 points} (43.9\%\,$\to$\,51.6\%), more
than the gap between the \#1 and \#2 \emph{models}. \sm{Both are preprints: GBP ``not certified by peer review''; DDB prints no venue.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\section{GeneBench-Pro --- simulate, so you can grade judgement}
% ###########################################################################

\begin{frame}{GBP --- the motivation: benchmarks weren't testing the hard part}
\why{The paper exists to close a gap. Real scientific analysis is far more complex than prior benchmarks measure --- and there are too few representative ones.}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\begin{block}{What prior benchmarks test}
\centering\footnotesize
curated dataset\\ $\downarrow$ \\ a well-defined, routine analysis \\ $\downarrow$ \\ a result
\end{block}
\centering\sm{The method is \emph{given}. This measures \textbf{execution}.}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
\begin{block}{What real scientific analysis is}
\centering\footnotesize
data of \textbf{unknown quality}\\ $\downarrow$ \\ EDA / QC / preprocessing \\ $\downarrow$ \\
modelling \& inference $\rightleftarrows$ \textbf{diagnostics} \\ $\downarrow$ \\ a decision-relevant conclusion
\end{block}
\centering\sm{The method must be \textbf{chosen}. This measures \textbf{judgement}.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{quote}\small\itshape
``Its core challenges stem not from the execution of analytical workflows, but from the importance
of scientific intuition or \textbf{`research taste'}: chains of judgment calls about what question the
data can support, what data to include, which estimand or model is appropriate\ldots{}''
\end{quote}
\src{GeneBench-Pro, p.\,2 --- verbatim}
\pause
\smallskip
\centering\sm{Sample collection is no longer the limiting factor --- \textbf{analysis is}. So a good
benchmark measures the exact step an AI agent could most help with.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{GBP --- characteristics}
\why{129 problems in genomics and translational biomedicine. But the unit they are built from is the thing to remember.}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \textbf{129 problems}, \textbf{10 primary domains}, 21 terminal subdomains.
  \item \textbf{Fully simulated} data --- the causal structure is \emph{known}, and nothing can be memorised from the literature.
  \item Docker, \textbf{no internet}, one fixed harness, a deliberately minimal prompt.
  \item Staged files look like what an analyst receives: \sm{batch effects, sentinel values, missingness, mixed ancestry --- the defects are \emph{deliberate}, and they are the point.}
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\begin{block}{The central construct --- the \emph{decision point}}
\footnotesize
\begin{quote}\itshape
``substantive inferential forks where a plausible wrong choice leads to a qualitatively different
downstream answer''
\end{quote}
\src{p.\,7 --- \textbf{3--13} per problem, median \textbf{6}}
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{$\sim$Six places, per problem, to take a defensible-looking wrong turn}
And the errors \textbf{cascade}: an incorrect choice at any stage propagates downstream and the agent
never recovers the correct target. \textbf{This is the idea I took wholesale for Part 2}: plant the
fork, know where the wrong path lands --- then you can say not just \emph{that} the agent was wrong,
but \textbf{which wrong}.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{GBP --- the justification: build it synthetically, for full control}
\why{The one design decision everything clever about GBP falls out of. It is how they avoid the two ways a long-horizon benchmark silently breaks.}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{alertblock}{Two ways these benchmarks break}
\footnotesize
\textbf{1. Too many defensible paths.} Multi-step questions built on \emph{messy historical data} have
no single correct route. One analyst's cutoff is as defensible as another's --- so the score reflects
the \textbf{author's arbitrary preference}, not model skill.
\smallskip

\textbf{2. Too numerically insensitive.} If the answer barely moves, an agent can make a
\textbf{fundamental error} and still pass.
\end{alertblock}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{exampleblock}{GBP's answer: simulate the data-generating process}
\footnotesize
Because they \textbf{know the full causal structure}, they can:
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \textbf{tune} the complexity of each problem;
  \item ensure \textbf{reasonable subjective choices still pass};
  \item \textbf{verify by ablation} that plausible-but-wrong analyses \emph{fail};
  \item \textbf{audit traces} for information leakage and unintended shortcuts.
\end{itemize}
\end{exampleblock}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\centering
\textbf{Getting the right answer depends on the right analytic \emph{pathway}} --- not on matching an
author's preference, and not on exploiting a shortcut. \sm{(GBP \S2, paraphrased; principles in their Table 1.)}
\end{frame}

\why{How a single problem is built, checked, and signed off. This is the ``image'' worth showing.}

\centering

\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{The external review is the part you cannot generate}
\footnotesize
\textbf{11 domain experts} --- \emph{graduate students, postdoctoral researchers, industry scientists,
and professors}. \textbf{84 candidate problems reviewed, 82 kept} in the suite. Each reviewer assessed:
\medskip

\centering\textbf{is it realistic? \quad is the target answer identifiable? \quad are the methods and estimators appropriate?}
\smallskip

\raggedright Feedback drove \textbf{prompt/data fixes} and the \textbf{redesign of problems whose
targets were not sufficiently identifiable.} \src{GeneBench-Pro, pp.\,7--8 --- ``External Scientific Review''}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{GBP --- grading: the subtlest choice in either paper}
\why{Easy to skim past. It is the difference between a benchmark that works and one that is silently broken.}

You simulated the data, so you know the true parameter you dialled in. \textbf{Grade against that? No.}
\medskip
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{alertblock}{The naive way (broken)}
\footnotesize
Simulator knob: true effect $\beta = \mathbf{0.30}$. But the staged files are a \emph{finite sample};
in \emph{those files} a \textbf{flawless} analyst recovers $\hat\beta = \mathbf{0.283}$ --- sampling noise.
\smallskip

Grade against $0.30 \pm 0.01$:\quad\rd{a perfect analysis is marked WRONG.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{exampleblock}{What GBP does (right)}
\footnotesize
Grade against \textbf{the estimate a correct analysis can actually recover from the staged files} ---
the \textbf{recoverable target}, \emph{not} the hidden generating parameter.
\smallskip

\centering\gd{Now the grader tests the \emph{analysis}, not the dice.}
\end{exampleblock}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{And the grade itself is \textbf{binary, deterministic Python} --- \emph{no LLM judge anywhere}}
\emph{``an agent that executes several intermediate steps correctly but returns the wrong
decision-relevant answer \textbf{has not successfully automated the analysis}.''} (p.\,14)
\smallskip

\centering\textbf{Partial credit measures effort. Binary measures usefulness.}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\textbf{28.7\%} is an \emph{average over problems}. The best mainline model scores \rd{zero across all

$3.7 \to 14.4 \to 22.5 \to 24.4 \to 26.8 \to \mathbf{28.7}$ --- an $8\times$ gain, but \emph{high}$\to$\emph{max} is only $+1.9$.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{GBP --- limitations}
\why{Two, and both bound how far you can read the numbers.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \rd{\textbf{OpenAI is evaluating OpenAI's own models.}} The leaderboard is topped by GPT-5.6
        (Sol / Pro), authored by the same lab. Judge-free grading protects the \emph{scores}, and they
        argue hardening tasks \emph{with} GPT biases \emph{against} it --- fair. But the \textbf{choice of
        headline model and the framing are in-house}.
  \pause
  \medskip
  \item \rd{\textbf{No human baseline.}} There is a \emph{time} reference (10--40 expert-hours per problem),
        but \textbf{no human \emph{accuracy} to read 28.7\% against}. Is an expert 90\%? 60\%? The paper
        does not say.
\end{itemize}
\pause
\vfill
\begin{alertblock}{So what the numbers actually tell you}
Not ``how close to a scientist'' --- there is no scientist in the table. Only \textbf{how good these
models are at \emph{these particular} tasks}, relative to each other. That is still a great deal, but
it is a narrower claim than the headline invites.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\section{DrugDiscoveryBench --- real tasks, an expert's playbook}
% ###########################################################################

\begin{frame}{DDB --- what it measures, and the bet it makes}
\why{The second paper trades GBP's simulated certainty for real-world grounding. Same story, opposite construction choice.}

\begin{block}{Early-stage drug discovery is a long, high-attrition funnel}
\centering\footnotesize
\textbf{target ID} \; $\to$ \; \textbf{hit ID} \; $\to$ \; \textbf{hit-to-lead} \; $\to$ \; \textbf{lead optimisation}
\smallskip

\raggedright\sm{DDB measures how reliably frontier \textbf{coding agents} do the multi-step
\emph{computational} and \emph{information-retrieval} work of this pipeline. It \textbf{stops before
candidate selection} --- DMPK, toxicology, formulation and clinical work are out of scope.}
\end{block}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{DDB's bet: pay experts to \emph{author} truth on real data}
\textbf{82 tasks written by pharmaceutical scientists}, each anchored to \textbf{one real artifact} ---
\sm{a granted patent, a PDB structure, a published paper, or a database record.} Each resolves to a
\textbf{single verifiable answer} --- an integer, a float, a SMILES/InChIKey, a ranking, or a short table.
\medskip

\centering\textbf{Where GBP \emph{simulates} to own the truth, DDB \emph{pays experts} to author it.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{DDB --- characteristics and key metrics}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\centering\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Expert-authored tasks} & 82 \\
Research capabilities covered & 7 \\
Lifecycle stages spanned & 4 (+adjacent) \\
Total weighted rubric criteria & 920 \\
\midrule
Biomni functions / domains & 226 / 22 \\
Data-lake files & 76 \\
Preinstalled scientific packages & 117 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\src{DrugDiscoveryBench, Table 1}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{exampleblock}{A representative task \sm{(lead optimisation)}}
\footnotesize
Two KRAS G12C co-crystal structures (\code{6USX}, \code{6UT0}). \emph{Why is compound B more potent?}
\smallskip

The agent must fetch both PDB entries, superimpose them, find the residue whose conformation shifts,
and confirm it contacts compound B. \textbf{Answer: \code{THR58}.}
\end{exampleblock}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{The contrast that will matter}
\centering
\textbf{GBP:} no internet, plain scientific stack. \qquad \textbf{DDB:} internet on, a 226-function
domain toolbox, 120 min/task.\\
\sm{More realism, more capability --- and, as we will see, more ways for the evaluation to leak.}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\end{alertblock}

\begin{frame}{DDB --- evaluation methodology}
\why{How a real-data, open-ended answer gets scored --- and the experiment that isolates \emph{planning} from \emph{execution}.}

\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \textbf{Presentation --- Harbor format.} A task prompt $+$ a shared footer pointing at the
        on-container references, the data lake, and the requirement to write only the final answer to
        \code{/workspace/answer.md}.
  \item \textbf{Harnesses.} Native where available (\textbf{Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI}); \textbf{mini-SWE-agent} as the universal cross-model fallback.
  \item \textbf{Reasoning effort.} Swept low / medium / ceiling \sm{(xhigh for GPT, max for Opus, high for Gemini)}. Every cell is \textbf{3 runs averaged}, 120-min timeout.
  \item \textbf{Grading.} An \textbf{LLM judge (GPT-5.4)} scores \code{answer.md} against the author's
        \textbf{weighted rubric}; \textbf{pass $=$ 100\% of the \emph{outcome} criteria}. \sm{Cross-checked vs Sonnet 4.6 and Gemini 3.5 Flash: $\kappa=1.0$ on binary labels.}
\end{itemize}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{Expert-method recovery --- planning vs execution}
Give three frontier agents the expert's \textbf{playbook} \sm{(the step sequence and which tools ---
\emph{never} the answer)}. \textbf{76 of 82} tasks already fall with no hints (best-of-many); the
playbook then lifts the remaining unsolved to \textbf{80 of 82 (+1 near-pass)}.
\medskip

\centering\emph{``execution is within reach for today's agents should they be given the expert workflow.''} \sm{(p.\,14)}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\textbf{3. What separates models is \emph{unguided high-level planning}} --- the recovery experiment
flips the unsolved tasks once the plan is supplied. \textbf{The gap is planning, not execution.}

\begin{frame}{DDB --- limitations}
\why{The scores flow through a judge, the taxonomy through single runs, the headline through an inclusion criterion --- and the internet was on.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \rd{\textbf{The judge has never been shown a human.}} Every number flows through \textbf{one LLM
        judge (GPT-5.4)}; the only check is against \emph{two other LLMs} ($\kappa = 1.0$, binary labels).
        Perfect agreement is \emph{suspicious}, not reassuring --- and the default judge is the \textbf{same
        family} as the leaderboard winner (GPT-5.5).
  \pause
  \item \rd{\textbf{The 54\% failure taxonomy is one run per cell.}} \emph{One run per (model, task)},
        226 model-failure runs --- no repeats, no error bar, in a paper whose own results show agent
        runs are enormously noisy.
  \pause
  \item \rd{\textbf{The headline hint result is near-circular.}} Solvability-given-the-playbook was an
        \emph{inclusion criterion} (Gate 4). The benchmark contains, by construction, tasks solvable with
        the playbook. \sm{And ``80/82'' is an \emph{oracle} across many runs; the best single agent is 51.6\%.}
\end{itemize}
\pause
\smallskip
\centering\sm{\textbf{And the internet leaked.} Gemini CLI found \emph{``preliminary results\ldots{}
released on the Scale Labs Blog''} and returned the answer --- and it is \textbf{\#2 at 50.0\%}. Excluded
from \emph{one} qualitative table; the aggregate is never audited.}
\end{frame}

\section{How these agents fail --- and what each failure demands}
% ###########################################################################

\begin{frame}{Over half of failures are not coding failures}
\why{DDB classified 226 failing runs into root causes. This table is what a builder actually needs --- and each row earns a mechanism in Part 2.}

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrp{0.50\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Failure mode} & \textbf{Share} & \textbf{Their definition} \\
\midrule
\rd{Domain reasoning} & \rd{54.0\%} & ``applies an incorrect scientific premise or misinterprets the data it has, \textbf{even though its inputs and tools are correct}'' \\
Derivation error      & 18.6\% & right approach, wrong calculation \\
Retrieval             & 16.4\% & wrong source, or never reads a provided file \\
Constraint            & 7.5\%  & violates an explicit instruction in the prompt \\
Final-answer slip     & 3.5\%  & every step right, reports the answer wrongly \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\src{DrugDiscoveryBench, Table 3 --- $n=226$ failing runs}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{Read the top row again}
\textbf{Over half of all failures are not coding failures.} The tools worked. The Python ran. The data
was right there. \sm{The next five slides make each failure concrete --- and name the Part 2 mechanism it
earns.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Failure 1 --- it notices, and then does nothing about it}
\why{GBP's central diagnosis. If you remember one thing from Part 1, remember this.}

\begin{quote}\itshape
``models often complete substantial portions of the workflow but exhibit a consistent gap between
\textbf{noticing} and \textbf{acting}\ldots{} As a result, models often select wrong estimators or
persist on initially plausible but incorrect analysis paths.''
\end{quote}
\src{GeneBench-Pro, abstract, p.\,1 --- verbatim}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{The intuition, in one sentence}
The model \emph{sees} the problem. It runs \code{df.describe()}. It observes the sentinel values. It may
even print a comment about them. \textbf{Then it cleans the column and proceeds with the analysis it had
already decided on.}
\medskip

\centering\textbf{The observation never reaches the decision.}
\end{block}
\bridge{a \textbf{Findings Ledger} --- \emph{enforce the observation}. A noticed problem becomes an open
obligation, and the exit is blocked until it is discharged.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Failure 2 --- the question quietly degrades}
\why{The one I find most alarming, because nothing crashes and the arithmetic is correct.}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{block}{DDB --- the melanoma task}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Asked:} which gene is the melanoma marker?\\
Mid-trajectory the qualifier \emph{``melanoma''} silently falls out. The ranking becomes a
\emph{gene-wide} variant count. \textbf{PTEN} wins on sheer volume. \sm{(Truth: CDKN2A.)}
\end{block}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{block}{GBP --- a denominator trap}
\footnotesize
The estimand is a probability over \textbf{the entire roster}. The agent standardises over
\textbf{tested partners only} --- a different denominator, a plausible answer, and the wrong one.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{The pattern}
\textbf{A constraint or a population silently drops out of the question partway through}, and every step
after that is a correct analysis \emph{of the wrong question}. GBP names the end state exactly:
\emph{``A statistically valid final model is applied to the wrong data or population, on the wrong
scale, or on the wrong conceptual level.''} \sm{(p.\,6)}
\end{alertblock}
\bridge{a \textbf{Question Contract} --- write the estimand, population, units and constraints down as
structured state, and \emph{re-show it every turn}, so decay becomes visible.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Failure 3 --- the right method, applied to the wrong object}
\begin{block}{DDB --- the net-charge task}
The chemistry is \textbf{correct}. The method is \textbf{correct}. It is executed on the neutral
\textbf{reference} molecule instead of the \textbf{bound ligand}. Answer: \textbf{0}. Truth: \textbf{$-$1}.
\end{block}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{The part that should worry anyone building a self-checking agent}
The agent ran \textbf{three internal consistency checks}. \emph{``All three independent checks agree''}
--- all three run \textbf{on the wrong molecule}.
\medskip

\centering\textbf{Internal consistency is not validity.}
\end{alertblock}
\pause
\smallskip
\sm{A verifier that reads the agent's own narrative will \emph{agree} with the agent's narrative.
Verification has to come from \emph{outside} the trajectory.}
\bridge{a \textbf{fresh-context validator} --- a \emph{different} model that never sees the agent's
reasoning, only the contract, the code, and what it printed.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Failure 4 --- its own code printed the right number}
\why{My favourite failure in either paper, because it is so cheap to prevent.}

\begin{quote}\large\itshape
``its own code printed the correct group-level count of 1, but the final tally used the atom-level
count of 2\ldots{} It reported 8 interactions instead of 7.''
\end{quote}
\src{DrugDiscoveryBench, p.\,27 --- verbatim}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{itemize}
  \item The analysis was \textbf{right}. The code was \textbf{right}. The output on screen was \textbf{right}.
  \item \rd{The number in the answer was not the number in the output.}
\end{itemize}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{exampleblock}{This is a \emph{15-line regex} away from being impossible}
An LLM reviewer \emph{might} catch this. A deterministic check --- \emph{``every number you report must
appear verbatim in the stdout of code that actually ran''} --- catches it \textbf{every time, for free}.
\end{exampleblock}
\bridge{\textbf{Evidence Grounding} --- \emph{ground the results}. Regex every reported number against
real stdout; reject the answer if a number was never printed.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Failure 5 --- nobody checks the answer at the end}
\begin{quote}\itshape
``\textbf{The last chance to catch the slip is at the final answer}: a human who had misread the task
the same way would look at the result, recognize it as a meaningless response to the user's actual
goal, and backtrack. \textbf{None of the failing models caught this.}''
\end{quote}
\src{DrugDiscoveryBench, p.\,13 --- verbatim}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{What that looks like in practice}
Claude Opus answers \textbf{BRCA2} --- a \emph{breast}-cancer gene --- to a \textbf{melanoma} question.
Any domain expert glancing at that output would stop dead. \textbf{The agent submits it.}
\end{block}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{One diagnosis, from two labs and two domains}
\emph{``the agents knew which database to query and how to compute the property\ldots{} But somewhere
along the execution they \textbf{drop a constraint, commit too early, fail to backtrack}.''} \sm{(DDB, p.\,12)}
\medskip

\centering The bottleneck is not code, not knowledge, not context length. It is \textbf{procedure} ---
\textbf{and procedure is something an engineer can build.}
\end{alertblock}
\bridge{finishing is a \textbf{gated action} --- an LLM judge must \emph{approve} the draft before
\code{submit\_answer} is allowed to return.}
\end{frame}

\section{The design: an LLM, a loop, three ledgers, a gate}
% ###########################################################################

\begin{frame}{The assignment, and the thesis}
\begin{block}{Part 2, Option A}
Give an agent \textbf{a question in English} and \textbf{a folder of data files}. It must explore the
data, choose an approach, write and run code, \textbf{check its own intermediate results}, and return a
\textbf{structured answer}.
\end{block}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{The design thesis --- straight out of Part 1}
The base model \textbf{already writes competent pandas}. The interesting engineering is not
``make it code''. \centering\textbf{It is making it \emph{keep the thread}.}
\end{alertblock}
\pause
\medskip
Part 1 says the agents drop exactly \textbf{three} things. So make each one \textbf{explicit state the
agent is not allowed to drop}:
\medskip

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{What gets dropped} & \textbf{The failure} & \textbf{The mechanism} \\
\midrule
\textbf{the question} & the melanoma qualifier evaporates & \textbf{Ledger 1 --- Question Contract} \\
\textbf{the finding}  & notices the sentinel, ignores it   & \textbf{Ledger 2 --- Findings Ledger} \\
\textbf{the number}   & printed 1, reported 2              & \textbf{Ledger 3 --- Evidence Grounding} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\medskip

\raggedright\sm{Do not spend the budget making the model smarter. Spend it making it \textbf{structurally
impossible} to drop the thread.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The base --- what an agent actually is}
\why{Strip the mystique. There are exactly three ideas, and all three are small.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The LLM is a pure function.} \code{messages} $\to$ \code{message}. No memory, no state,
        no ability to act. Everything else is something \emph{you} build around it.
  \pause
  \item \textbf{Tool calling is not the model running code.} The model emits a \emph{structured request}
        --- ``please call \code{run\_python} with this string''. \textbf{Your program executes it} and
        appends the result to the transcript. The model never touches the machine.
  \pause
  \item \textbf{The agent loop is a \code{while} loop.} \sm{(ReAct, Yao et al.)}
\end{enumerate}
\medskip
\begin{lstlisting}
while step < budget:
    msg = llm(transcript, tools)          # think
    if msg.tool_calls:
        result = execute(msg.tool_calls)  # act
        transcript.append(result)         # observe
    if submitted: break
\end{lstlisting}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{That is the whole agent --- about 40 lines}
\textbf{Everything interesting in this design is in what surrounds that loop}, and every piece of it was
earned from a failure in Part 1.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The architecture}

\begin{frame}{Three tools --- and why not more}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.62\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{run\_python(code)} --- executes in a \textbf{persistent namespace}, returns stdout +
        truncated traceback. Variables survive across steps, exactly like Jupyter cells.
        \smallskip

        \sm{\textbf{One general tool beats $N$ specific ones.} Anything pandas can do, the agent can do ---
        no capping it at whatever verbs I anticipated.}
  \item \code{note\_finding(...)} --- the ledger.
  \item \code{submit\_answer(...)} --- the \textbf{gated} exit.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.38\textwidth}
\begin{alertblock}{That is all}
Every additional tool is \textbf{surface area to misuse} --- and Part 1 shows the models are \textbf{not
failing for lack of affordances}.
\medskip

\sm{DDB gave its agents \textbf{226 functions}. They still scored 51.6\%, and 54\% of the failures
happened with \emph{correct inputs and correct tools}.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{The single most important context rule}
\centering\textbf{The model never sees the data.}\\
It sees \textbf{metadata}, \textbf{summaries}, and \textbf{the output of code it wrote}.
\end{block}
\smallskip
\sm{This is what makes the design \textbf{scale-invariant}: a 200{,}000-row dataframe and a 200-row one
cost the \emph{same} tokens, because in both cases the model looks at \code{df.dtypes}, \code{df.head(3)},
and whatever it chose to \code{print}. The dataframe lives in the executor process.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Addition 0 --- the detector, and the idea the design rests on}
\why{Before the model is called even once, plain Python looks at the data.}

\begin{block}{A deterministic profiler runs at turn 0}
Shape, dtypes, \code{head(3)}, null counts, cardinality --- \emph{and it runs detectors}: sentinel values,
duplicate identifiers, numeric-looking strings, group imbalance.
\end{block}
\pause
\medskip
Then the move that matters. What it finds is \textbf{not printed as a helpful note}.

\medskip
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}
\node[draw, rounded corners, fill=unired!8, draw=unired, text width=11.2cm, align=center, inner sep=6pt]
{It is \textbf{registered as an \emph{open obligation}} --- before turn one.\\[3pt]
\large\textbf{Information can be ignored. An obligation cannot.}};
\end{tikzpicture}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{The division of labour the whole design rests on}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{deterministic code} & finds the mechanical problems, and makes them \textbf{un-ignorable} \\
\textbf{the model}          & decides what they \textbf{mean} for \emph{this} question \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{block}
\smallskip
\sm{It is not a straitjacket: on the response-rate task the agent looks at the pre-seeded \code{-999}
finding and \textbf{dismisses} it --- correctly, because that column never enters the calculation.
\textbf{We do not force a conclusion. We force the observation to \emph{reach} the decision.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Ledger 1 --- the Question Contract}
\why{Attacks Failure 2 --- the melanoma scope-drop; DDB \emph{Constraint} (7.5\%); GBP's ``wrong population / wrong scale''.}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{lstlisting}
class QuestionContract(BaseModel):
    estimand:   str   # the exact quantity
    population: str   # WHICH ROWS. the denominator.
    units:      str   # percent? fraction? log?
    constraints: list[str]  # every stated filter
    premises:    list[str]  # what the QUESTION
                            # assumes -- must check
    question_is_precise: bool   # REQUIRED
    ambiguities: list[str]
\end{lstlisting}
\smallskip
\sm{Pinned into context \textbf{every turn}. Shown to the verifier at submit time.}
\medskip

\begin{exampleblock}{Example --- a filled contract}
\scriptsize
\code{estimand}: mean biomarker change, baseline$\to$wk 12\\
\code{population}: \textbf{arm A only}, completers\\
\code{units}: raw assay units \sm{(not \%)}\\
\code{premises}: [``arm A was randomised'']\\
\code{question\_is\_precise}: \gd{true}
\end{exampleblock}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\begin{alertblock}{Two fields the \emph{evaluation} demanded}
\footnotesize
\textbf{\code{premises}} --- a question can be \emph{wrong}.
\emph{``Which of the \textbf{four} sites had the highest response rate?''} --- there are \textbf{three}.
\emph{``\textbf{Why} do women respond better?''} --- \textbf{they don't}.
\medskip

Without this field the agent answered both, \emph{fluently}, laundering a false premise into a fact.
\textbf{0/3 $\to$ 3/3.}
\medskip

\textbf{\code{question\_is\_precise}} --- a \emph{required boolean}, and that is the whole trick. With an
optional \code{ambiguities} list the agent left it empty \textbf{every single time}.
\medskip

\centering\textbf{A field the model \emph{may} leave empty is a field the model \emph{will} leave empty.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Ledger 2 --- the Findings Ledger}
\why{Attacks Failure 1 --- the notice--act gap directly: GBP's headline, and DDB's 54\% domain-reasoning bucket.}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
Every time the agent notices \emph{anything}, it must log it:
\begin{lstlisting}
note_finding(
  observation = "biomarker_x has 47 values "
                "of exactly -999.0",
  implication = "missing-data sentinels, not "
                "measurements; drag mean ~30%",
  status      = "open",  # open|acted|dismissed
)
\end{lstlisting}
\smallskip
\begin{alertblock}{The rule that makes it bite}
\centering
\code{submit\_answer} is \textbf{hard-blocked} while \emph{any} finding is \code{open}.
\end{alertblock}
\smallskip
\sm{To close one: \gd{act} (point at the code step) or \textcolor{unigrey}{\textbf{dismiss}} (say why it
doesn't affect the estimand). The \code{implication} field is where the \emph{propagation} happens ---
the step both papers watch it skip.}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Example --- a run's ledger, the audit artifact}
\centering\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.20\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{status} & \textbf{what it noticed $\to$ what it did} \\
\midrule
\gd{ACTED} & \code{biomarker\_baseline} has 88 \code{-999}s $\to$ excluded before any mean \\
\gd{ACTED} & 48 \code{patient\_id}s appear twice $\to$ kept the last row per patient \\
\gd{ACTED} & \code{arm} imbalanced on \code{severity} $\to$ stratified; conditional effect \\
\textcolor{unigrey}{\textbf{DISMISS}} & batch B on a shifted scale $\to$ never enters a \emph{rate}; no effect \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{block}
\smallskip
\centering One is an \textbf{oracle}. The other is a \textbf{colleague}. \sm{$\sim$40 lines, one tool.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Ledger 3 --- Evidence Grounding}
\why{Attacks Failure 4 --- DDB's Derivation errors (18.6\%) and Final-answer slips (3.5\%).}

\begin{block}{The rule, stated in the system prompt as an absolute}
\centering\textbf{Every number you report must have been printed by code you ran.}\\
\sm{Estimating is not a permitted operation.}
\end{block}
\medskip
And then it is \textbf{enforced deterministically}, not requested politely:
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item Regex the numbers out of the answer's \code{evidence} list.
  \item Normalise --- strip commas and percent signs, compare at 4 significant figures, \textbf{also test
        $\times100$ and $\div100$} for the percent-vs-fraction confusion.
  \item Require set containment against the \textbf{concatenated stdout of the whole run}.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
\begin{lstlisting}
REJECTED: the value 0.62 in your evidence never appeared in any executed
output. Compute and print it before citing it.
\end{lstlisting}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{No LLM is involved. It cannot be talked out of it.}
DDB documents this exact failure \emph{in a frontier model} --- ``printed 1, reported 2''. An LLM
reviewer \emph{might} catch that. \textbf{A regex catches it every time, for free, in fifteen lines.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The gated exit --- finishing is an \emph{action}, not a default}
\why{Attacks Failures 3 and 5 --- ``the last chance to catch the slip is at the final answer. None of the failing models caught this.''}

\code{submit\_answer} runs four gates \textbf{in order --- cheapest and most deterministic first}:

\medskip
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}clp{0.52\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule

\section{How would I know it works?}

\begin{frame}{The evaluation \emph{is} the design}
\why{Both supplied papers are, fundamentally, evaluation papers. So I steal their protocol --- and say so.}

\begin{block}{How I built the benchmark --- the simplest honest version}
\begin{enumerate}\footnotesize
  \item I \textbf{simulated two datasets} following GBP's principles, and \textbf{used a third, held-out} one.
  \item I asked \textbf{Claude to author 28 tasks} over them --- lookups, aggregations, and \emph{planted traps}.
  \item I grade with \textbf{binary, programmatic metrics}: ground truth is a pandas callable \emph{only the grader} ever invokes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{block}
\medskip
\begin{itemize}\footnotesize
  \item \code{penguins.csv} --- real, clean, familiar. The agent should score 100\%, and a guardrail should buy \emph{nothing}.
  \item \code{trial.csv} --- \textbf{synthetic clinical data with a known data-generating process}, built so plausible-but-wrong analyses give \emph{materially different} answers.
  \item \code{sales.csv} --- \rd{a \textbf{held-out domain}}. E-commerce, not medicine. \textbf{Never designed against.}
\end{itemize}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{GBP's third principle is the one people skip --- and it saved me}
For every task I \emph{assert} the naive answer and the correct answer differ by \textbf{far more than the
grading tolerance}. That guard \textbf{immediately killed two of my own tasks} --- a median robust to the
sentinels I planted, and an age question independent of its own filter. Both looked reasonable. Both
graded nothing. \textbf{The assertion found them; I did not.}
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The ablation study --- switch off one mechanism at a time}
\begin{block}{}
\centering
\textbf{4{,}480 runs} $\cdot$ 28 tasks $\cdot$ 3 domains $\cdot$ 8 configs $\cdot$
\textbf{20 runs per cell} $\cdot$ \textbf{$\sim$\$16} $\cdot$ \textbf{0 crashes} $\cdot$ full agent \textbf{87\%}
\end{block}
\medskip
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Remove this} & \textbf{Pass} & \textbf{$\Delta$ vs full} & \textbf{Verdict} \\
\midrule
\emph{(nothing --- the full agent)} & \textbf{87\%} & --- & \\
\midrule
\rd{the deterministic data briefing} \sm{$\leftarrow$ \textbf{the DETECTOR}} & \rd{60\%} & \rd{$-27\%$} & \rd{\textbf{HURTS}} \\
\emph{every guardrail at once} & 67\% & $-20\%$ & \textbf{HURTS} \\

\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{Removing \emph{twenty lines of pandas} hurts more than removing \emph{every gate combined}}
I built this design around \textbf{gates}. The thing carrying it is the \textbf{detector} --- the mechanism
I spent the \emph{least} time on.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The conclusion the ablation forced}
\begin{block}{}
\centering\Large\textbf{A gate is only as good as the detector feeding it.}
\end{block}
\smallskip
\centering The gates were not \emph{wrong}. They were \textbf{redundant} --- the detector in front of them
was already doing the job. Remove the detector and the gates have nothing to gate.
\pause
\medskip
\begin{alertblock}{The objection I want to raise before you do}
\textbf{GBP says the opposite:} \emph{frontier} models \emph{consistently notice} --- the headroom is in
\emph{acting}. \centering\textbf{Because we are not talking about the same model.}
\smallskip

\raggedright GBP's subjects are \textbf{GPT-5.6-class}. My agent is \textbf{Qwen3-30B-A3B} --- chosen
\emph{precisely because it is weak}. For a frontier model, \code{df.describe()} runs and the sentinel
\textbf{is} spotted --- noticing is the solved half. For a 30B model at \$0.10/1M, \textbf{noticing is
genuinely not reliable}, so the profiler \textbf{substitutes for a capability the base model lacks}.
\end{alertblock}
\pause
\medskip
\begin{block}{}
\centering\textbf{The notice--act gap is a frontier-model problem. The \emph{notice} gap is a small-model problem.}
\smallskip

\raggedright\sm{Which half of the scaffolding earns its keep depends on which half of the job your base
model already does for free --- which is an argument for \textbf{owning an eval harness}, not a list of
best practices. The right answer changes when you change the model.}
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\end{block}

\begin{frame}{Backup --- the instrument I built to stop fooling myself, lied}
\why{I criticised GBP's error bars in Part 1. Then I made the same mistake, and only caught it by accident.}

I ran \textbf{the identical benchmark twice}, either side of a change I had \textbf{proved was inert}.

\medskip

\begin{tabular}{@{}lrlc@{}}
\toprule

\textbf{Same code. Same tasks. Opposite conclusions.} With \textbf{10 runs/cell} the bootstrap resamples

produced. \textbf{A bootstrap gives you the variance of the data you \emph{have}}, and nothing about the
data you did not collect.
\pause

If a verdict flips between two runs of the same experiment, it was never a verdict. \textbf{It was
weather.} \sm{Two of the seven mechanisms' signs flip; only the \textbf{briefing} ($-27\%$, 4-pt spread)
I defend without hedging.}
\end{alertblock}

\begin{frame}{Colophon --- where the numbers come from}
